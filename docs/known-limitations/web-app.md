# The web app

Panels, the sidebar, window widths, reloading after a deploy and the installed app. Part of [Known limitations](../known-limitations.md).

- **The old `/settings/users`, `/settings/ai` and `/settings/groups/<id>` paths redirect.** `/settings/users` and `/settings/ai` go to `/admin/users` and `/admin/ai` — site administration is its own panel, opened from **Site admin** in the user menu (admins only); `/settings/groups/<id>` goes to the group's panel at `/groups/<id>`, and `/settings/groups/<id>/roles` to its **Roles** page. `/admin/auth` and `/admin/versions` go to `/admin/login` and `/admin/storage`. Bookmarks to the old paths still work; a non-admin following an `/admin/*` link, or one of the old `/settings/users` and `/settings/ai` paths, ends up on the home page.
- **Closing a panel navigates forward, not back.** Opening Settings, a group's panel or Site admin pushes a new browser history entry rather than replacing the current one; closing it (Esc or the ✕; on a phone, **‹** on its list of sections or tapping the strip above it; on a wider window clicking outside doesn't close it) navigates back to wherever you were. If you then press your browser's own Back button, it reopens the panel instead of leaving the page you were on before. Switching to another page inside the panel (from its list of pages) also adds a history entry, so it takes one extra Back press for the panel plus one for each switch you made, including going back to a page you had already visited.
- **On a window 640 pixels wide or wider, the pinned name column can cover part of a row's first button when a table doesn't fit its panel.** (On a phone, below 640 pixels, tables show each row as a card and don't scroll sideways.) How wide a window that takes depends on the table, its content and the language: measured with the panel open and typical content, the Users table is still wider than its panel at about 1280 pixels, AI providers and Sign-in services at about 1160 and 1080, **Site admin → Groups** at about 1020, the AI Actions table at about 1000, Invitations at about 920, and Storage plans at about 880; other tables can overflow too. A table too wide for the panel scrolls sideways inside its own frame while its name column stays pinned on the left. In the tables with four buttons a row — **Actions** in **Site admin → AI agents** and **Sign-in services** in **Site admin → Sign-in** — the name column can still cover part of the leftmost button once the table is scrolled all the way to the right, and a click on the covered part lands on the name instead. Scroll back a little, or move to the button with Tab: when a button or switch that the name column partly covers gets keyboard focus, the table scrolls so it is no longer covered. Only the top-level name column is pinned: the models listed under each AI provider scroll with the table, names included, and so does what is shown under a row — such as a sign-in service's callback URL, a warning, or **Add model** at the top of a provider's models — so when a table doesn't fit you may need to scroll right to see all of it.
- **On a narrow window or a touch screen, the icon buttons at the bottom of the sidebar in selection mode have no visible name.** Move to, Copy to, Remove from favorites, Restore, Delete permanently and the delete button show only an icon. Their names show only as a mouse tooltip (a greyed-out Move to or Copy to shows the reason it can't be used instead) or to screen readers.
- **On a window 1280 pixels wide or more, the sidebar and the version history panel sit beside the note and can crowd its header.** With both dragged to their widest (sidebar 480 pixels, panel 560), a window around 1300 pixels wide or narrower leaves the note too narrow for the share button and `⋮` at the right of the header, and they can be cut off, especially when others are viewing the note. Drag either one narrower to get them back; double-click the gap beside the sidebar or the panel's left edge to go back to the default width. On a narrower window both float over the note instead and take no room from it.
- **The Recent tab doesn't reorder for edits made elsewhere until the note list refreshes.** It follows the note you have open as soon as its header shows the new edit time, but an edit someone else (or an AI client) makes to another note moves that note up only after the list is fetched again — when the window regains focus, the page reloads, or you create, move or delete a note.
- **The Favorites tab doesn't update right away in another window or on another device.** Adding a note to your favorites or removing it shows up in a second window, or on another device, the next time the list is fetched there: when that window regains focus, when you open a note, or when the page is reloaded.
- **A floating sidebar or version history panel covers part of the note.** Between 640 and 1279 pixels the sidebar (opened with the button at the left of the header) and the version history panel (the clock button at the bottom right) float over the note; neither pushes it aside. The panel's top edge is placed just under the header, and under the version preview bar while a version is being previewed, so the buttons at the right end of that bar stay in reach; opening or closing a preview therefore moves the panel's top edge by the height of the bar. Side by side, the right column of a preview is kept clear of the panel, and a wide panel on a narrow window can leave very little room for the preview while the panel is open. With a small browser font size the window can leave less room than the panel's width; the panel is then shown narrower and the width you chose is kept.
- **Notes with the same title have sidebar `⋮` buttons that sound the same to a screen reader.** Each is named after its note ("Note actions for *title*"), so several notes called, say, "Untitled" give several identical "Note actions for Untitled" buttons; tell them apart by the row they sit in.
- **A wide table on a phone can show a fade on its right edge when it overflows by only a fraction of a pixel, and can show it late.** In browsers that animate with the scroll position, the fade appears once about half a pixel or more is hidden; in the others it appears only when more than 1px is hidden. In those others the fade can also be out of date after a table gets wider without its text or cells changing, for example when an image in it finishes loading or a column is resized, until you scroll that table.
- **While several notes are being moved into a group or copied from the sidebar, or folders deleted or removed, the confirmation can't be closed — if the server stops answering, reload the page.** It stays open until the last request comes back, and the web app gives a request no time limit; the **Move to…** and **Copy to…** dialogs for a single note behave the same way. A request already sent may still have gone through, so check the notes after reloading.
- **"This looks like a failed chunk load" is decided by matching error messages.** When the note page's on-demand code chunk fails to load (#66), the automatic recover-by-reload path only fires if the error message matches a known wording: the phrasing Chromium, Firefox and Safari use for a failed dynamic import, Vite's own "Unable to preload CSS" message (what a failed load of this chunk actually produces most of the time, since the chunk ships with a CSS file that is fetched first), and the MIME-type error a `try_files`-style reverse proxy produces when it answers a missing asset with `index.html`. If a future browser or bundler version rewords any of these, nothing breaks — the failure just lands on the generic error screen with a retry button instead of reloading automatically. The trade-off fails toward the safe side (never toward a blank page or a reload loop), which is why message matching was accepted at all. Separately, the *nested* chunks the note page loads on demand after mounting (currently syntax highlighting) are outside this mechanism entirely: they fail as unhandled promise rejections, not render errors, so a deploy rotation can leave code blocks silently unhighlighted until the next full reload.
- **The note page auto-reloads at most once per successful load, per tab.** The reload that recovers from a deploy rotation (#66) spends a per-tab budget tracked in `sessionStorage`, re-armed only when the note chunk actually loads successfully — so a persistently broken deploy shows the error screen rather than reloading forever, and a tab whose storage is unavailable (some private-browsing modes) skips the automatic reload entirely and goes straight to the error screen with its retry button. Duplicating a tab copies the spent budget along with it; each tab re-arms on its own next successful load.
- **Scrollbars are deliberately low-contrast.** The scrollbar thumb's contrast against its track falls short of WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 in every state but one (light: resting ≈2.1:1, hover ≈2.9:1; dark: resting ≈2.9–3.0:1) — a conscious visual-subtlety trade-off, not an oversight. The one state that clears the bar is dark-mode hover, at ≈3.9:1. Scroll affordance is still available through other cues: content gets visibly clipped at the viewport edge, and the thumb still occupies space in its track even where its contrast is hard to make out.
- **In Chromium-based browsers, Knotebook can only be installed as an app when it is served over HTTPS (or opened from `localhost`).** Chromium-based browsers (Chrome, Edge, Android Chrome) offer **Install** only on a secure origin, so on the trusted-LAN plain-http setup in [Deployment prerequisites](../self-hosting.md#deployment-prerequisites) they don't offer it. On an iPhone, Safari does offer **Add to Home Screen** on a plain-http site, and the added app opens in its own window; other browsers and iOS versions haven't been checked. See [Installing Knotebook as an app](../install-app.md) (#336).
- **The installed app doesn't work offline.** There is no service worker, so the app needs the server just like a browser tab: opened without a connection, it shows the browser's or the system's error page. A note that was already open keeps the usual offline behaviour of a tab — edits stay in memory and sync when the connection returns, and are lost if the app is closed first (#336).
- **The installed app's top bar is briefly light on each start for someone who uses dark.** The colour of the app window's top bar goes through three stages: before the page loads, it uses the light colour from the manifest (the manifest has no dark variant), so on Android the launch screen is light even for someone who uses dark; once the page's HTML is parsed and before the app has started, it follows your device's light or dark setting; once the app has started, it follows the theme you pick in Knotebook's settings. So if you pick dark in Knotebook while your device is set to light, the top bar stays light until the app has started (#336).
- **On a public share page, the browser's own menu can still offer to install Knotebook.** The page holds back the browser's automatic install prompt there, because installing from a public link only opens the sign-in page, but a reader can still choose **Install** or **Add to Home Screen** from the browser's menu, and iOS always offers **Add to Home Screen** (#336).
